Korea’s highways go toll-free for Chuseok, Sept. 24–27

Driving in Korea over Chuseok? The government is waiving highway tolls for four days, from September 24 through 27. Here’s the part worth knowing before you reach the tollgate: Hi-Pass users should keep their transponders on, and drivers using staffed lanes should follow the usual ticket procedure.

Trandory ·2026.09.20
01

Four toll-free days: September 24–27

Under the Korean government’s 2026 Chuseok livelihood package announced September 1, highway tolls nationwide will be waived from September 24 through 27. The waiver covers all four holiday days, not just Chuseok Day.

Some locally managed toll roads have announced separate waivers, but don’t assume every paid road follows the national-highway rule. Check the relevant operator if your route includes a municipal or privately managed road.

02

Keep Hi-Pass on

If you use Hi-Pass, leave the transponder powered on and drive through the Hi-Pass lane as usual. If you use a conventional lane, take a ticket when entering and hand it over when exiting. In other words, the fee changes; the normal tollgate procedure does not.

03

Check traffic before you leave

Free tolls do not mean lighter holiday traffic. Korea Expressway Corporation’s ROAD PLUS service and navigation apps provide live traffic information, so it is worth checking your route just before departure and planning fuel and rest stops around congestion.
