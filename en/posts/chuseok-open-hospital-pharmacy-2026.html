Finding an open hospital or pharmacy in Korea over Chuseok

A fever or a missing prescription can become much harder to handle during a holiday. Regular hours shown on a map may not apply, so here are the official lookup services to check before you leave.

Trandory ·2026.09.19
In this guide
  1. Hospitals and pharmacies
  2. Pharmacy-only search
  3. When to call 119
01

Use E-Gen to search hospitals and pharmacies together

The National Medical Center’s E-Gen emergency medical portal lets you choose a date and area to look for clinics, hospitals and pharmacies operating over the holiday.

A search result does not guarantee that registration is still open. Departments and last check-in times vary, and schedules can change on the day. Call the facility to confirm that it can see you and ask when registration closes. Treat ordinary map-app hours as a reference, not final confirmation.

02

For pharmacies, cross-check Holiday Pharmacy

If you only need a pharmacy, the Korean Pharmaceutical Association’s Holiday Pharmacy service also provides date-and-area searches. If you expect a prescription, check the hospital and pharmacy schedules separately.

The services are primarily in Korean. Travelers who cannot make a Korean-language call may want to ask hotel staff or a local contact to help confirm the hours.

03

For urgent symptoms, call 119 instead of searching

There is a difference between finding routine holiday care and needing immediate help. If someone has reduced consciousness, difficulty breathing, severe chest pain or bleeding that will not stop, do not lose time choosing a hospital yourself. Call 119 for emergency instructions and dispatch.

For non-urgent care, search E-Gen, cross-check pharmacies and call before leaving. In an emergency, skip those steps and call 119. This guide explains how to find care; it is not a diagnosis or a substitute for medical treatment.
