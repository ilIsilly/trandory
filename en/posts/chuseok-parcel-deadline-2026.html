Shipping a Chuseok gift in Korea? Don’t wait for the deadline

Chuseok falls on September 25 this year. If you’re sending a gift within Korea, the headline cutoff is useful—but giving yourself a few extra days is the safer plan.

Trandory ·2026.09.18
01

September 22 is the general-area shipping cutoff

According to logistics-industry schedules reported on September 15, CJ Logistics, Hanjin and Lotte Global Logistics will pause parcel operations from September 24 through 26. For most destinations, September 22 is the stated cutoff for parcels intended to arrive before the holiday break.

That is not a guarantee that any parcel handed over on the 22nd will arrive before Chuseok. Convenience-store counters, local agents and pickup services can have different collection times, and holiday volume adds uncertainty. If the arrival date matters, shipping a few days earlier is the better bet.

02

Jeju and some remote areas move up to September 21

For Jeju and certain island or mountain destinations, the cutoff is a day earlier, September 21. Refrigerated, frozen or otherwise time-sensitive items may face separate restrictions, so check the specific service you’re using before dropping them off.

03

Holiday e-commerce delivery follows different rules

The three major parcel networks are expected to resume normal operations on September 27. Retailers with their own delivery networks follow different calendars: Coupang plans to keep its delivery system running through the holiday, while Kurly and SSG.com are scheduled to pause some deliveries on Chuseok Day, September 25.

For online orders, the practical move is to check the promised arrival date on the product page immediately before checkout. During a holiday week, the fulfillment method matters more than the retailer’s name alone.

Source checked
