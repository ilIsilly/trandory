Chuseok 2026 train tickets: check payment before you pack

Regular holiday reservations must be paid by September 15; priority reservations by September 18. Here is what to check as of September 13, including remaining seats.

01

A reservation still needs payment

Chuseok is a major family holiday in South Korea, and many people travel home by train. If you live in Korea or are visiting during the holiday, a successful reservation can feel like the hard part is over. Before you turn to work schedules, family visits, or packing, check that both legs of your trip are paid for.

KORAIL’s September 11 announcement says payment opened at midnight on September 12. Regular holiday reservations must be paid by September 15, while priority reservations for eligible passengers have a September 18 deadline. Unpaid reservations are canceled after the deadline and allocated to waiting-list applicants in order.

Check the reservation category, payment status, and displayed deadline for each ticket. A relative’s priority reservation may have a different deadline from yours. If you book a newly available seat, follow the payment deadline shown for that new reservation; do not assume the holiday advance-booking deadline applies to every purchase.

02

No ticket yet? Look at remaining seats and waiting lists

The regular advance-booking period has ended, but remaining seats went on sale at 3 p.m. on September 11 through the KORAIL+ app, KORAIL’s official website, station counters, and ticket machines. Availability changes, so you need to check your actual route and departure time.

If a train is full, see whether the booking screen offers a waiting list. Joining one does not guarantee a seat. Do not plan around a supposed late-night hour when canceled tickets will definitely appear; use the official availability display and allocation notices.

If your schedule is flexible, agree on acceptable departure times with your travel companions before searching. An evening train might work, but only if you can still reach your final destination after arriving. This is more useful than collecting reservations for dates you cannot realistically use.

03

Helping family travel? Check the booking category too

KORAIL’s advance-booking notice covers trains running September 23–27. That operating window does not mean all five dates are public holidays for everyone. Match travel dates to your actual time off.

The notice also says existing KORAIL members transfer automatically to integrated membership, while people registered only with SR need to complete the enrollment or conversion process. If you have not used the service recently, check the account associated with your reservation.

Eligible priority-booking passengers who have difficulty paying online can use the rail customer center at 1588-7788 within Korea or a station counter. Tickets paid for by phone must be issued at a station counter before boarding, with identification. If you are helping a parent, make sure they know about that extra step.

04

A short weekend check can simplify the trip

Start with payment status, then check outbound and return dates and the departure station. Share your arrival plan with the people meeting you. If you are still looking for tickets, decide which times are workable and check remaining seats or waiting-list options.

People in their 30s do not all spend the holiday the same way. You may be traveling alone, helping parents, or bringing children. Use the checks that fit your plans, then review official service updates again shortly before departure.
