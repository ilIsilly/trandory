iPhone 18 Pro preorders: who should upgrade, and who should wait?

Preorders opened in Korea on September 12. Before spending nearly two million won, here’s what actually changed — and whether those changes solve a problem you have today.

01

This year, the price deserves attention before the specs

The iPhone 18 Pro starts at ₩1.99 million in Korea for 256GB, while the Pro Max starts at ₩2.19 million. Preorders opened at 9 p.m. on September 12, with the phones scheduled to arrive on September 18.

At that price, the useful question is not simply whether the new phone is better. It is whether it fixes something that bothers you about the phone you already own. If your current Pro model is only a year or two old and still works well, there is less reason to rush.

Apple also announced its first foldable, the iPhone Duo, starting at ₩3.29 million in Korea. Preorders begin October 16, followed by an October 23 release. If a foldable is genuinely appealing to you, waiting a month to compare both devices in person makes sense.

02

The camera and battery are the easiest upgrades to understand

The 48MP main camera now uses a four-step variable aperture, giving photographers more control over light and depth of field. That is more meaningful if you regularly shoot travel, food or video than if your camera roll is mostly quick snapshots.

The A20 Pro chip is paired with a next-generation vapor chamber. Apple is emphasizing sustained performance and battery life, and its Korean store lists up to 43 hours of video playback for the Pro lineup. Real-world battery life varies with usage, so that figure is best treated as a comparison point rather than a promise about your day.

Siri’s new AI features in iOS 27 are another headline feature, but they are still rolling out in beta and availability varies by language and feature. If AI is your main reason for upgrading, check support for the features and language you actually use before buying.

03

The best upgrade test is still your current phone

If your phone is three or more years old, its battery is noticeably worn, or photography is a major part of how you use it, the iPhone 18 Pro is easier to justify. If you own a recent Pro and cannot name a daily frustration, waiting for long-term reviews is perfectly reasonable.

Before chasing a preorder promotion, separate the device price, monthly service cost and trade-in value. Carrier discounts can look large until plan requirements or card-spending conditions are included.

My own rule would be simple: write down what is wrong with your current phone. If the answer is camera or battery, this generation has a clear case. If nothing comes to mind, you probably do not need a preorder countdown to make the decision for you.
