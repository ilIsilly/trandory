Shopping before Chuseok? Korea’s grocery discounts end before the holiday

Chuseok starts next week, but some of Korea’s biggest grocery-support programs wrap up before the holiday itself. Here are the dates worth knowing.

Trandory ·2026.09.16
01

Government-backed grocery discounts run through September 23

Korea’s Ministry of Agriculture, Food and Rural Affairs is running a national discount program for domestically produced agricultural and livestock products from September 3 through September 23. The government allocated KRW 59 billion to the program and says eligible products at large and midsize supermarkets can be discounted by as much as 40%.

Supply is also being increased for 13 major holiday staples, including apples, pears, Korean beef and eggs. The planned volume is 163,000 metric tons over the three weeks before Chuseok, about 1.6 times the usual level. “Up to 40%” is not a blanket discount, though; the exact item and rate depend on the retailer and promotion.

02

Traditional-market benefits have an earlier cutoff

Discounted agricultural vouchers are being sold at 20% off, with the general-sale window running September 14–20. Separately, participating traditional markets are holding an Onnuri gift-certificate rebate event from September 16–20. The agriculture ministry says shoppers can receive a rebate equal to 30% of qualifying agricultural and livestock purchases.

The rebate is available at 300 participating markets, not every traditional market in the country, so it is worth checking participation before making a special trip.

03

The two dates to remember: September 20 and 23

If you are planning a traditional-market run, September 20 is the key cutoff for these programs. For the broader supermarket discount support, September 23 is the last day. Korea’s Chuseok holiday period begins September 24, so waiting until the holiday itself may mean missing the government-backed grocery discount window.

And the usual shopping rule still applies: a discount only saves money on something you actually need. Checking the refrigerator first and making a short list is probably more useful than chasing every promotion.
