Camping in Korea this fall? Check these before you book

Cooler nights make September a tempting time to camp. Before picking a site from photos alone, here’s what’s worth checking in Korea’s official campground information.

Trandory ·2026.09.15
01

More fall camping inventory is coming online

On September 15, Korean travel platform NOL announced new booking access for 25 campgrounds operated by local governments, covering camping, car camping and glamping. We’re treating that as a same-day travel-industry signal, not as an official real-time search ranking.

For a second check, the Korea Tourism Organization’s Go Camping database lists registered campgrounds with details such as location, operating season, site type, pet policies and safety equipment. KTO also cautions that on-site conditions can differ from the listing, so anything essential to your trip should be confirmed with the campground.

02

Look past the pretty photos

Start with the facilities you’ll actually use: restrooms and showers, power access, parking, ground surface, pet rules and equipment rental. Check when the listing was last updated, too. Policies for extra vehicles, pets or rentals can change, and a quick confirmation before departure can prevent an awkward arrival.

03

Cool nights make safety rules more relevant

KTO’s campground safety materials separately cover fire, electricity, gas and first aid. Its inspection checklist includes rules around open flames in tents, electrical equipment, extinguishers and carbon-monoxide alarms.

If you’re bringing a heater or cooking gear, don’t assume it’s permitted just because it’s common camping equipment. Check the campground’s current rules first. A good fall campsite is less about finding the most famous view and more about finding a place that fits the way you actually camp.
